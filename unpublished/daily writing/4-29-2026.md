# Running as a new hobby

What spurred this whole desire to get better at running was just an attempt to fix some of the malaise I feel right now.
I feel somewhat lost -- not unhappy I wouldn't say, but that makes it harder to define.  Just lost.  I've spent so much 
of my life with some kind of tangible goal that I am reaching toward (...I guess this didn't start til after high school.  
get into berklee.  get into prep school.  get into west point.  be a pro weightlifter.  software.... it gets muddy after
weightlifting.  that fucking sport, for the very limited benefits, it really did a number on me), that now it feels like this
goal-centric way of living is just...pointless. 

I am happy at playstation, but as the work becomes more intense I realize, I dont really want to hvae to work that hard.  
Couple that with the fact that AI can do most of what I do, far better and  faster, I am questioning how much I want 
"programmer" to actually be the source of my identity.  Requiring this identity is a whole issue in and of itself 
but that will be the topic of a different stream of consciousness. What I want to think about right now is why I am
fantasizing about a future where I'm a really great runner.  what will it fix?

Nothing really.  What is the fantasy?  its so minimal, I don't even really have that much motivation to doing this other 
that "it will be good for my health".  But I feel like so many of my insecurities are rooted in my current habits that I 
am attracted to the idea of those things not being so important to me.  My chubby face... if I deadlift 600lbs, then my 
back will be so large that it wont matter.  If I bench 400 lbs, my upper body will be so muscular that it wont matter 
what my face looks like.  These arent logical but they've directed me for so long. How is running any different?

For starters, its a completely different aesthetic.  Trying to "be huge" is a lot of work, and it doesn't feel healthy
That with my recent blood work showing that I'm 1 tick below being pre-diabetic, and already having high LDL and high 
cholesterol makes it feel like the nutrition to be huge is slowly killing me. Maybe I won't care so much about my 
male pattern baldness, and my shoulder size if I'm just a little bit leaner (I realize that this is t he same problem
just in a different direction). but it does feel like it could be healthier. 

In truth that's the main motivator.  I want my labs to be healthy, but I also want my athleticism back.  I feel so
slow and useless.  I'm arguably the strongest I've ever been, but I hate all the asterisks.  I can't sleep without 
rolling on a ball for 30 mintes.  I have trouble breathing because I'm just 240lbs.  I cant really jump without 
feeling it in my knees.  I've really taken weightlifting to the extreme and never found a balance to come back down.  
I just fell down, and now its showing that I'm really not the athlete I was before. 

maybe running for me will be more like a proxy of health and a new era of my identity as an athlete.  Its hard to 
keep using that word when I already so many thoughts regarding whether or not even NEEDING an identity is a 
positive thing or another silent killer.  But if it is good, maybe this will be a good change. My hesitance is that I've 
run before.  I used to kind of do it alot and I know what the injuries can be.  I want to approach this as an 
adult with money.  Not as a poor child, which is how I've basically approached every hobby I've ever had. 
There's no reason to do that anymore because now I see the effects of not spending the investment up front
to actually get the most out of something.  

It will start with once a week, but I want to get a running coach to check my form, and from there, work with a 
PT to start fixing all the dumb shit about my current physiological state. Competition, marathons all that 
stuff is ubiquitous with a culture im not a fan of.  I may not participat in those things but I dont want 
my perception of that archetype to keep me from doing something which might be really positive and really
heathy for my life.  

I have nothing to lose at this point.  I fdidnt want to lose weight because I kind of liked this idea of being big,
but I'm the biggest I've ever been and I still feel small.  It won't ever change, so I might as well chase an 
aesthetic that looks good in clothes and naked.  I might as well chase an aesthetic which promotes health and 
confidence and not one which is relegated to only having confidence in the gym, or having just come from the gym

That being said I will still focus many efforts on the qualities of an athlete.  As much as Alec Blenis irritates 
me I think his training methodologies are rtight up my alley in terms of prioritizing stability, quickness, 
leanness, atheleiticism, endurance and strength.
